How Real Annual ROI is calculated

Incremental IRR vs. the earliest claiming age shown. Claiming later is treated like an investment: the "cost" is the benefit foregone while waiting, and the "return" is the extra lifetime household Social Security that results. We solve for the annualized real rate r at which the net present value of that incremental cash flow is zero. A positive ROI means delaying beats claiming early, in today's dollars.

Taxable Social Security

IRS two-tier formula

Provisional income (PI) = all non-SS income + 50% of household Social Security.

Married filing jointly:
Exempt PI < $32,000 → $0 taxable SS
Tier 1 $32k–$44k → min(50%×(PI−$32k), 50%×SS)
Tier 2 PI > $44k → min($6,000+85%×(PI−$44k), 85%×SS)

Single (after one spouse passes):
Exempt PI < $25,000 → $0
Tier 1 $25k–$34k → min(50%×(PI−$25k), 50%×SS)
Tier 2 PI > $34k → min($4,500+85%×(PI−$34k), 85%×SS)
⚠️ Frozen thresholds: these dollar limits were set in the 1980s–90s and have never been COLA-adjusted, so more of your Social Security tends to become taxable each year as other income rises with inflation.
⚡ SS torpedo: in the phase-in zones, every extra dollar of non-SS income can pull up to $0.85 more of Social Security into taxation on top of itself — an effective marginal rate well above your regular bracket.

Total Income Tax

Ordinary income + qualified dividends & LTCG

Ordinary rates apply to wages, pension, rental, IRA withdrawals, non-qualified dividends, short-term gains and taxable Social Security. Qualified dividends and long-term gains stack on top and are taxed at preferential 0/15/20% rates.

Simplified calculation — no AMT, net investment income surtax, itemized deductions, or credits.
