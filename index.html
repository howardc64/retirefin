Annual household spending, held flat in today's dollars. Each year it is paid, in this order, from (1) household income (wages, Social Security, pension, rental, IRA RMDs), then (2) the dividends of the brokerage portfolios that have Pay expenses checked (any dividends not needed are reinvested), then (3) selling those portfolios' assets — each sale realizes long-term capital gain (LTCG) in proportion to the portfolio's unrealized gain (see each portfolio's cost basis). Income tax (tax drag) is also paid this way, so selling shares to cover it can realize more LTCG — the model iterates until the tax and the sales agree. The AUM fee (below) and the Medicare IRMAA surcharge are paid the same way. Only portfolios with Pay expenses checked (off by default; IDGT or not) supply those dividends and sales.
A capital-loss carryforward available to offset realized long-term gains (LTCG). Each year, available SCGL first eliminates that year's realized LTCG (dollar-for-dollar, before tax) until it's used up; only LTCG left over after that is taxed. Shown net-of-SCGL on the Annual Household Income chart and in the Total Tax calculation.
Advisory / management fee. AUM balance = the sum of the balances of every brokerage portfolio, pre-tax IRA and Roth IRA that has its AUM box checked (portfolios must also be enabled, inside their age range and funded). The fee is that balance × the percentage, or a fixed dollar amount per year (entered in today's $, held flat in nominal terms so it shrinks in today's $ with inflation). It is an expense paid like living expenses — household income, then dividends, then asset sales — and is charged on the AUM accounts pro rata to balance, but as an expense it is paid only by the portfolios with Pay expenses checked, not by the account it is charged on. The Medicare IRMAA surcharge is also always paid as a household expense, so it needs no setting here.

Social Security Tax

Taxable Social Security

The taxable portion of Social Security is determined using the applicable filing-status thresholds below, then taxed as ordinary income — the resulting dollars of tax are the actual Social Security Tax (SST).

Married filing jointly:
Exempt PI < $32,000 → $0 taxable SS
Tier 1 $32k–$44k → min(50%×(PI−$32k), 50%×SS)
Tier 2 PI > $44k → min($6,000+85%×(PI−$44k), 85%×SS)

Single (after one spouse passes):
Exempt PI < $25,000 → $0
Tier 1 $25k–$34k → min(50%×(PI−$25k), 50%×SS)
Tier 2 PI > $34k → min($4,500+85%×(PI−$34k), 85%×SS)
⚡ Compounding effect: every additional dollar of income that increases the taxable portion of SS is itself taxed, and can push more SS into taxability, compounding the effect.

Total Income Tax

Ordinary income + qualified dividends & LTCG

Ordinary rates apply to wages, pension, rental, IRA withdrawals, non-qualified dividends, short-term gains and taxable Social Security. Qualified dividends and long-term gains stack on top and are taxed at preferential 0/15/20% rates, plus a 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT) once MAGI clears the (un-indexed) $200k single / $250k married threshold — shown as "QDIV/LTCG + NIIT."

Simplified calculation — no AMT, itemized deductions, or credits.
